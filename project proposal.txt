Project Proposal: Visualizing Life Expectancy and Fertility (1964–2013)
Project Title:
From Large Families to Longer Lives: Global Relationship Between Fertility and Life Expectancy (1964–2013)

1. Introduction
This project aims to explore how life expectancy and fertility changed between 1964 and 2013 across countries and continents. Rather than only examining whether these variables changed, this project focuses on how their relationship evolved over time and whether countries followed similar or divergent development paths.
Using an interactive data visualization, this project will examine global demographic change and allow users to explore how countries moved from patterns of high fertility and low life expectancy toward lower fertility and longer lives.

2. Research Question
Main Research Question:How did the relationship between fertility and life expectancy change across countries and continents between 1964 and 2013?
This question focuses on both temporal change and structural change. It examines not only how fertility and life expectancy changed independently, but also how the relationship between the two evolved over time.

Sub-question 1: Which continent experienced the fastest overall demographic transition between 1964 and 2013?
Sub-question 2: When did countries experience their demographic transition?
Sub-question 3: What was the typical GNI per capita when countries completed the demographic transition?

3. Expected Findings
The project is based on the expectation that most countries moved from a demographic pattern of high fertility and low life expectancy toward low fertility and high life expectancy between 1964 and 2013.
However, this transition is not expected to be uniform. Some regions likely experienced faster development than others, and certain countries may show temporary reversals or deviations due to major crises such as conflict, famine, or epidemics.

The expected overall pattern is:
* Global fertility rates decreased over time
* Global life expectancy increased over time
* The relationship between fertility and life expectancy became more strongly negative
* Countries became more similar overall, though regional inequalities remained
* Some countries significantly deviated from the general trend

4. Visualization Design
Main Visualization: Interactive Bubble Scatter Plot by Bokeh.
This will serve as the central figure of the project.

Sub-visualization 1: Bar plot by Plotly
Show which continent experienced the fastest overall demographic transition between 1964 and 2013.

Sub-visualization 2: World map by Plotly
By clicking on a country on the map you can see the data of the country, and countries are projected in different color for different year range.

Sub-visualization 3: Bar plot by Plotly
Show GNI of the transition year of countries in ranges.
